## Adaptive Biotechnologies
- **HQ:** Seattle, WA (1551 Eastlake Ave. E, per search-result listing; source: https://ce.advocatehealth.org/content/adaptive-biotechnologies)
- **Status:** Current ACS account, so this is an expansion play.
- **Foundation / giving program:** No foundation found. There is a corporate social responsibility (CSR) program, which launched with a charitable contribution to STEM Paths Innovation Network (SPIN) for the SPIN Girls Fellows program (girls of color in King County, grades 11–12, STEM careers). Source: https://www.adaptivebiotech.com/corporate-social-responsibility/ (search snippet; page itself blocked). There is also an Independent Medical Education (IME) grants program run by Medical Affairs: https://www.adaptivebiotech.com/call-for-independent-medical-education-ime-grants/
- **Focus areas:** Equity in STEM/biotech, communities where employees live and work, DEI, and sustainability (CSR page snippet, source above). IME grants cover education for healthcare professionals (HCPs) about its immune medicine platform and MRD testing (source above).
- **Patient-support adjacency:** "Adaptive Assist" gives payment help to qualified patients using clonoSEQ (blood cancer MRD test). Source: https://www.clonoseq.com/adaptive-assist/. The company engages with blood-cancer patient groups; for example, a Cutaneous Lymphoma Foundation "Industry Spotlight" (https://www.clfoundation.org/industry-spotlight-adaptive-biotechnologies). The details of that relationship are *unverified*.
- **Typical grant size:** unverified (none published).
- **Geographic limits:** CSR examples are King County / Seattle (SPIN). Formal limits are unverified.
- **How to apply:** IME grants go through the Medical Affairs page above (process details unverified because the page was blocked). No public charitable or sponsorship request form was found, so community and sponsorship asks appear to be by relationship or invitation (unverified).
- **Open RFPs / deadlines:** None found.
- **Past cancer/ACS gifts:** Its core business is cancer diagnostics (clonoSEQ, the FDA-cleared MRD test for lymphoid cancers; https://www.adaptivebiotech.com/clonoseq-mrd-test/). Searches found **no public record** of ACS gifts, Relay For Life / Making Strides sponsorships, or LLS / Light The Night sponsorships. The existing ACS account relationship is known internally only. Press releases only cite ACS statistics (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/adaptive-biotechnologies-clonoseq-minimal-residual-disease-test-predicts-relapse-in-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma-patients-by-sensitively-detecting-circulating-tumor-dna-300059625.html).
- **Employee giving / matching:** unverified (not found).
- **Public grants / contact page:** https://www.adaptivebiotech.com/corporate-social-responsibility/ and https://www.adaptivebiotech.com/call-for-independent-medical-education-ime-grants/
- **Suggested Tier:** 1
- **ACS angle:** Adaptive's blood-cancer patients (myeloma, ALL, CLL, lymphoma) travel for long treatment courses. A Road to Recovery / Hope Lodge (Seattle) sponsorship, or an ACS helpline/navigation tie-in for MRD-monitored patients, would expand the existing account from event giving into mission-aligned patient support. The SPIN equity focus could also pair with ACS health-equity or research-workforce programs.
- **Timely hook:** On 2026-09-25 Adaptive publicized the new International Myeloma Society "definition of cure" that relies on MRD testing (https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/25/3369095/0/en/adaptive-biotechnologies-highlights-role-of-highly-sensitive-mrd-testing-in-new-international-myeloma-society-definition-of-cure.html). This is a good conversation opener about survivorship.

## Expedia Group
- **HQ:** Seattle, WA
- **Foundation / giving program:**
  - **Made to Travel Fund** (national/global): grants to nonprofits "removing barriers to travel" for underserved or underrepresented travelers. It gave more than $1.5M in its first year (2022–23). Sources: https://www.expedia.com/newsroom/made-to-travel-fund/ ; https://www.phocuswire.com/expedia-grants-nonprofits-giving-tuesday
  - **Employee-directed community grants:** Local employees nominate and vote on local charities twice a year. This was documented for the Bellevue HQ era, so current status is unverified. Source: https://www.expedia.com/stories/expedia-inc-employees-choose-charitable-grant-recipients/
  - Candid lists an "Expedia Group, Inc. Corporate Giving Program": https://fconline.foundationcenter.org/fdo-grantmaker-profile/?collection=grantmakers&activity=result&key=EXPE008
  - The umbrella strategy is called Open World (social impact and sustainability): https://www.expediagroup.com/en-us/about-us/impact-and-sustainability
- **Focus areas:** Accessible and inclusive travel (disability, LGBTQ+, underrepresented travelers), plus community economic advancement. Search found no health or cancer focus. Sources: the Made to Travel release above; 2023 Global Impact Report release (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/expedia-group-releases-its-2023-global-impact-report-demonstrating-actions-and-progress-aligned-with-its-open-world-social-impact-and-sustainability-strategy-302137036.html)
- **Typical grant size:** Made to Travel grants reached $250,000 in at least one case (UnTours Foundation: https://untoursfoundation.org/expedia-awards-travel-grant-to-untours-foundation-through-new-made-to-travel-fund/). The typical size is unverified. In total, the company and employees provided $8.7M to partners and 60 nonprofits received grants (per the Global Impact Report summary; https://careers.expediagroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/expedia-group-global-impact-report-2025.pdf, which was blocked, so the reporting year is unverified).
- **Geographic limits:** Made to Travel is global/national. Community grants are local to offices.
- **How to apply:** Made to Travel appears to be invitation / selection-based. No open application was found (unverified).
- **Open RFPs / deadlines:** None found.
- **Past cancer/ACS gifts:** None found (a search for Expedia plus ACS, Hope Lodge or Relay For Life returned nothing).
- **Employee giving / matching:** 1:1 match, $25 minimum, $4,000 maximum per employee per year, for US and Canada employees, via Benevity (expediacares.benevity.org). Volunteer rewards also exist. Source: https://doublethedonation.com/matching-gifts/expedia-inc (third-party, so current figures are unverified). There is also an annual September "Month of Caring" volunteer event in more than 50 countries (Global Impact Report 2025, URL above).
- **Public grants / contact page:** https://www.expediagroup.com/en-us/about-us/impact-and-sustainability
- **Suggested Tier:** 2
- **ACS angle:** Hope Lodge and Road to Recovery remove travel and lodging barriers for cancer patients. That can be framed as "access to travel for care", a craftable fit with Made to Travel and a natural match for in-kind lodging or points donations. Employee matching and Month of Caring could drive Relay / Making Strides teams.
