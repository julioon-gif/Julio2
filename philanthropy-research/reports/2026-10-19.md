# PNW Corporate Philanthropy Scan: Week of Oct 19, 2026

**4 new companies, 14 changed.** This week I re-checked 14 entries from the Sep 28 scan on the funders' own pages and added 4 new companies. **(V)** means verified on the funder's own page or official filing; **(U)** means unverified.

## Top 3 actions (verified)
1. **Genentech: submit before Nov 26 (V).** Genentech's funding request system stops accepting charitable requests at 12:00 AM PT on Nov 26 and reopens Jan 4, 2027. Requests must arrive at least 60 days before the program starts, so after reopening it won't take anything starting before Mar 4. Community programs in Hillsboro and Portland get "additional consideration," and early diagnosis and patient support fit its "Equitable Care" area. Genentech is already an ACS national "Groundbreaker" partner of nearly 40 years, so coordinate with the national team first. https://www.gene.com/good/giving/corporate-giving
2. **Walmart Spark Good Local Grants: apply by Nov 30 (V).** Grants are $250–$5,000 per store, and ACS can have up to 25 applications open at once. https://walmart.org/how-we-give/program-guidelines/spark-good-local-grants-guidelines
3. **Premera: an existing ACS partner (V).** Premera's partner page lists ACS under "Healthier Communities, King County." Premera says it has worked with ACS "for more than two decades," and its employees joined Making Strides in 2026. Its grants are invitation-only, so confirm who owns this relationship internally and ask for an invited screening or navigation grant. https://wellbeyond.premera.com/partners/

## Corrections and changes
- **Premera, raised to Tier 1:** see above. Its focus areas are behavioral health and homelessness, healthier communities, and rural health.
- **Avista:** the earlier "quarterly" claim was wrong. It has one window per focus area, and Health & Human Services runs Jan 1–Feb 1 (V).
- **Boeing, lowered to Tier 3:** the Puget Sound Employees Community Fund funds capital items only and excludes national fund drives, medical research and large medical organizations (V). The Portland fund isn't accepting applications.
- **Wells Fargo, lowered to Tier 3:** grants are invitation-only (V). The ACS page that cited its past ACS honors has been removed, so those claims are now unverified. Check ACS's own records.
- **Bank of America:** its 2026 windows are confirmed (Basic Needs ran Feb 2–Mar 2), and Seattle, Spokane, Portland/Vancouver and Boise are eligible (V). 2027 dates aren't posted. The past $50K ACS rides grant is now unverified because its source page was removed.
- **CareOregon, lowered to Tier 3:** its 2026 grants are invitation-only and capped at $10K, and 2026 sponsorships are closed (V). The application window reported earlier belonged to a separate foundation focused on youth.
- **Kaiser Permanente WA:** it has an open event sponsorship application with posted cycles (V). The last cycle closed Oct 16, and the next isn't posted. Grants remain invitation-only.
- **Providence:** Spokane/Stevens sponsorships are rolling (up to $10K), and Spokane also offers fall community grants, dates not posted (V). Providence Oregon is closed to grants and sponsorships through 2026.
- **Blue Cross of Idaho:** it supports Camp Rainbow Gold, an Idaho camp for kids with cancer (V). Its 2026 employee fund has closed. The rolling-request process reported earlier could not be confirmed.
- **Moda Health:** it serves OR, AK, ID and TX, not WA (V). Its next deadline is Sep 1, 2027, and it matches employee gifts up to $1,250.
- **Pfizer:** "Change the Odds" is a $15M, three-year ACS initiative that started Feb 2024 (V). Renewal talks around Feb 2027 are the moment to push for PNW inclusion.
- **Adaptive Biotechnologies and Apree Health, lowered to Tier 2:** neither has a giving program, so these are partnership asks through the current accounts. Apree's HQ is Sandy, UT; its PNW presence is its Vera care centers in the Seattle area and Portland.

## New prospects
- **Washington Trust Bank** (Spokane, WA), Tier 2: it gives $3.8M+ a year across WA, ID and OR, mostly through sponsorships. Nonprofits reach it by contacting a banker. It cites a long commitment to lodging for families traveling for medical care, a natural fit for Hope Lodge and Road to Recovery.
- **Lamb Weston** (Eagle, ID), Tier 3: its foundation gives only to charities it selects (mostly food banks). It has given to the Tri-Cities Cancer Foundation, and matches employee gifts up to $1K.
- **Banner Bank** (Walla Walla, WA), Tier 3: no health focus. It prioritizes organizations where its employees volunteer.
- **Philips** (Bothell ultrasound site): no grant route, so not a fit for now.
